# Sample Dyad: Cross-Pursuit with Asymmetric Regulation

This example illustrates a classic **cross-pursuit dynamic** driven by Worth, Status, and Safety seeks, with escalation and withdrawal interacting asymmetrically.

---

## Profiles (0–60 scale)

### Person A (Husband)

- Withdraw (W): 42 (Hi)
- Escalate (E): 30 (Med)
- Negative Interpret (N): 30 (Med)
- Invalidate (I): 18 (Lo)

**Primary Seek:** Worth  
**Secondary Seek:** Status  
**Tertiary Seek:** Justice  

**Profile Summary:**  
Under stress, Person A withdraws quickly to manage overwhelm and protect worth. He retains moderate capacity to escalate and interpret intent, but struggles to hold boundaries under pressure due to low Invalidate capacity.

Withdrawal is the dominant regulation strategy.

Core questions under stress:
- *“Am I enough?”*
- *“Where do I stand?”*

---

### Person B (Wife)

- Withdraw (W): 30 (Med)
- Escalate (E): 42 (Hi)
- Negative Interpret (N): 34 (Med)
- Invalidate (I): 34 (Med)

**Primary Seek:** Status  
**Secondary Seek:** Threat (Safety)  
**Tertiary Seek:** Justice  

**Profile Summary:**  
Under stress, Person B escalates to restore position and safety. She assigns meaning to distance or ambiguity and is capable of holding her perspective, though not rigidly.

Escalation is the dominant regulation strategy.

Core questions under stress:
- *“Where do I stand?”*
- *“Am I safe?”*

---

## Collision Pattern

### Cross-Pursuit with Asymmetric Withdrawal

| Phase | What Happens |
|-----|---------------|
| **Trigger** | External stress or relational friction |
| **Initial Response** | B escalates (Hi-E); A withdraws (Hi-W) |
| **Seek Activation** | B’s Status seek reads withdrawal as dismissal |
| | A’s Worth seek reads escalation as failure |
| **Secondary Loop** | B escalates to regain position and safety |
| | A withdraws further to manage overwhelm |
| **Complication** | A’s Lo-Invalidate leads to capitulation rather than repair |
| **Initiation** | A's Lo Invalidate acts as a stealth conflict trigger

---

## Why This Pairing Escalates

### Worth–Status Feedback Loop
- Escalation threatens A’s sense of worth
- Withdrawal threatens B’s sense of status
- Each partner’s regulation strategy intensifies the other’s threat

---

### The Lo-Invalidate Trap (A)
- A struggles to hold his perspective under pressure
- Agreement is often compliance, not resolution
- Resentment accumulates quietly

---

## What This Looks Like

- Loud conflict cycles
- Pursuit followed by shutdown
- Repeated “Why won’t you engage?”
- Exhaustion without true repair

The conflict is visible, but the drivers are not.

---

## Seek → Give Opportunity

| Person | Seeking | Formation Move |
|------|---------|----------------|
| A | Worth, Status | Give **Status** early before withdrawing (“I hear you; this matters”) |
| B | Status, Safety | Give **Safety** early before escalating (“We’re okay; I want to understand”) |

Small moves early prevent large ruptures later.

---

## Notes

This example illustrates:
1. Cross-pursuit is driven by identity threat, not disagreement  
2. Low Invalidate capacity destabilizes repair  
3. Escalation and withdrawal are reciprocal regulators  
4. Mid-range capacities can collapse under pressure
