# Sample Dyad: Parallel Withdrawal with Control–Safety Tension

This example illustrates how conflict can escalate **without loud behavior**, driven by withdrawal, interpretation, and control-seeking.

---

## Profiles (0–60 scale)

### Person A (Husband)

- Withdraw: 42 (Hi)
- Escalate: 29 (Med)
- Negative Interpret: 19 (Lo)
- Invalidate: 30 (Med)

**Primary Seek:** Order (control)

Under stress, A withdraws to stabilize and reduce chaos.  
Withdrawal functions as a control strategy rather than avoidance.

---

### Person B (Wife)

- Withdraw: 56 (Hi)
- Escalate: 17 (Lo)
- Negative Interpret: 51 (Hi)
- Invalidate: 23 (Med)

**Primary Seeks:** Threat (safety), Order (control)

Under stress, B withdraws outwardly while escalating interpretation internally.  
Silence increases threat monitoring rather than calm.

---

## Collision Pattern

**Parallel Withdrawal with Asymmetric Meaning-Making**

- Both partners disengage early
- A experiences silence as stabilization
- B experiences silence as uncertainty and threat
- No corrective feedback loop forms

The same behavior produces opposite internal effects.

---

## Why This Is Risky

- No visible conflict → false sense of stability
- Interpretation accumulates privately
- Concerns surface late and heavily
- Withdrawal intensifies on both sides

The system destabilizes quietly.

---

## Seek → Give Opportunity

- **A** can give Safety through brief reassurance before withdrawing  
- **B** can give Order by naming uncertainty early, even imperfectly  

Early, low-intensity signals prevent late, high-load rupture.

---

*The quietest conflicts are often the most fragile.*
